# Page copy

Ready to paste. Replace `[City]`, `[Practice]`, `[Dr Name]` throughout.

The angle underneath all of it: **people don't avoid the dentist because of
price alone. They avoid it because they expect to be judged for how long it's
been.** Price is the stated objection; shame is the real one. Most dental ads
only answer the first.

---

## 1 · Hero

**Headline**
> New patient exam and X-rays for $59

**Subhead**
> No lecture about how long it's been. Just a clear look at where things stand,
> and what — if anything — actually needs doing.

**CTA button**
> Book my $59 exam

**Under the button**
> [Practice] · [City] · Most PPO plans accepted

First person on the button — *my*, not *your*. It reads as the visitor's own
decision rather than an instruction, and it consistently outperforms.

## 2 · Offer box

**Heading**
> What $59 covers

- Full dental exam with [Dr Name]
- Complete digital X-rays
- Oral cancer screening
- A written treatment plan, with prices, before anything is scheduled
- 45–60 minutes — not a five-minute look and a rebooking

**Under the list**
> Normally $285. No insurance needed. **Nothing owed on the day beyond the $59.**

That last sentence does more work than the price itself. The unspoken fear isn't
the $59 — it's walking out having agreed to $4,000 of work.

## 3 · Trust

> ⚠️ **Placeholder structure only. Do not publish invented reviews.**
>
> Before this page goes live, replace with real reviews — pulled from the
> practice's Google listing with permission. Each needs a real first name, a
> real city, and their actual words.
>
> If you're building this as a demo rather than for a practice, either use
> clearly-labelled sample text (`Sample review — replaced with the practice's
> real Google reviews on a live build`) or leave the section empty and say so on
> camera. A page with fabricated testimonials on a public URL is a real problem,
> not a shortcut.

**Section heading**
> What patients say

**Each card needs:** first name · city · 2 sentences in their own words · star
rating · a real photo or initials, never a stock headshot

**Pick reviews that answer objections**, not reviews that say "great service."
One about cost, one about anxiety, one about a long gap since their last visit.
Those are the three barriers on this page.

## 4 · What happens at your visit

**Heading**
> What actually happens

1. **You arrive and we take X-rays.** About ten minutes, and they don't hurt.
2. **[Dr Name] examines and talks you through what they see** — on the screen,
   so you can see it too.
3. **You get a written plan with prices.** Nothing is booked on the day unless
   you want it to be.
4. **You leave knowing where you stand.** Even if the answer is "nothing needs
   doing," which it often is.

Step 4 matters. "Nothing needs doing" is a real outcome and saying so out loud
removes the suspicion that the exam is a sales appointment in disguise.

## 6 · Location

**Heading**
> Finding us

- Address, embedded map
- Parking: one specific sentence. "Free parking behind the building, off [Street]"
- Hours
- Phone number, tappable

Specific parking detail is a small trust signal that reads as a real business
rather than a landing page.

## 7 · FAQ

**What if I need work done?**
> You'll get a written plan with prices on the day. Nothing is scheduled unless
> you decide to schedule it. You're free to take the plan and think about it, or
> get a second opinion.

**Will it hurt?**
> An exam and X-rays don't hurt. If you're anxious about dental visits, say so
> when you book — we'll go slower and explain each step before it happens.

**Do I need insurance?**
> No. The $59 covers the full visit whether you're insured or not.

**How long does it take?**
> Between 45 minutes and an hour. We don't double-book, so you won't be waiting.

**It's been years since I've seen a dentist.**
> Then this is exactly the appointment for you, and you're in more company than
> you think. We're not going to lecture you about it — we're going to tell you
> where things stand and what your options are.

That last answer is the highest-value copy on the page. It names the real
barrier, and almost no competitor will have written it.

## Voice notes

**Sentence case. No exclamation marks.** A dental practice writing in excited
marketing voice reads as an agency wrote it, because one did.

**Second person, present tense.** "You arrive and we take X-rays," not "patients
will be seen by our team."

**Name prices. Never say "affordable."** Vagueness about cost is what people are
afraid of.

**No emoji.** Not in this vertical, not for this demographic.

**Read the whole page out loud before publishing.** If a sentence is awkward to
say, it's awkward to read, and this page is mostly read on a phone by someone
who is already slightly anxious.
